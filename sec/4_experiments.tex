\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Data and metrics.}
We evaluate on SPAR-Bench~\cite{spar} using the 20-task configuration. Tiny contains 1,000 samples and Full contains 7,211. Overall is the unweighted mean of the 20 task scores. Low averages eight depth/distance tasks, Middle averages three view-change/matching/camera-motion tasks, and High averages nine relation/imagination/center-distance tasks. Task scores use the benchmark scale of 0--100 and include partial credit for numerical predictions. PoseScore equals Middle. Tiny is a smaller evaluation set and does not provide an independent replication of Full. Input interventions use a separate 600-sample development set.

\paragraph{Training.}
Balanced20 contains 100,000 samples, with 5,000 per category. The original training budget is 25,000 optimizer steps, corresponding to one epoch with micro-batch size 1 and gradient accumulation 4. Each arm then continues from its own 25K checkpoint for 5,000 additional steps, consuming 20,000 more samples for 1.2 epochs in total. The optimizer and RNG states are restored, the epoch sampler is advanced from the cumulative data cursor, and warmup is not restarted. AdamW uses weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping 1, BF16, and 100 initial warmup steps followed by constant learning rates of $2\times10^{-5}$ for language LoRA and $10^{-4}$ for new modules. LoRA uses rank 16, scaling alpha 32, and dropout 0.05. Training and development use a 50,176-pixel preprocessing budget; Tiny and Full use 230,400 pixels.

\paragraph{Comparisons and decoding.}
B0 is the original 3DThinker adaptation with 29,933,568 trainable language LoRA parameters. GENERIC and CGMF additionally receive RGB-D and measured calibration and each train 40,611,458 parameters. GENERIC retains camera embeddings in attention memory. All evaluations use seed0, greedy decoding, eight continuous latents, and a 4,096-token generation limit. We report every available 10K, 15K, 20K, 25K, and 30K Tiny/Full result. B0 was not evaluated at 20K; the missing scores are neither interpolated nor replaced with another checkpoint. Training uses the same settings across arms; GPU scheduling is an execution detail rather than an experimental factor.

\paragraph{Verification and provenance.}
We recomputed task scores from the stored sample scores and checked sample identities, protocol records, completion markers, and checkpoint manifest hashes. All evaluated samples remain in the denominator. B0 at 15K Full and CGMF at 30K Full each contain one empty answer; the other reported conditions contain none. Integrity records show no scoring exceptions, truncations, or deviations from eight latent steps. B0 at 10K, 15K, and 25K reuses historical results whose original source-code hash manifest is incomplete. The 30K B0 checkpoint is a new continuation of its historical 25K checkpoint, so this provenance limitation also applies to the inherited training trajectory.

The 20K evaluation node and 30K continuation were added after the original schedule was specified. We therefore report the continuation as a follow-up experiment, alongside all earlier checkpoints, rather than describe the complete trajectory as prospectively fixed. Retraining ablations and multiple-seed comparisons remain incomplete. No result is inferred from a missing evaluation.

\subsection{Main Results}
\input{sec/results_main}
At 30K, CGMF scores 48.530 on Full, compared with 50.345 for GENERIC and 49.489 for B0 (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Its differences are $-1.814$ points relative to GENERIC and $-0.959$ relative to B0. GENERIC exceeds B0 by 0.855 points. Tiny scores are 53.280, 54.296, and 53.016 for CGMF, GENERIC, and B0, respectively. The numerical CGMF advantage over GENERIC at 10K and 20K Full reverses at 25K and 30K. At 25K Full, the scores are 47.294 for CGMF, 49.052 for GENERIC, and 48.207 for B0. These comparisons do not support an overall advantage from the camera-specific bias and gate.

The paired analysis uses 5,000 task-stratified bootstrap resamples and nominal 95\% intervals. At 30K Full, the Overall interval is $[-2.577,-1.043]$ for CGMF minus GENERIC, $[-1.742,-0.184]$ for CGMF minus B0, and $[+0.144,+1.551]$ for GENERIC minus B0. The scene-cluster intervals for the same comparisons are $[-2.636,-0.990]$, $[-1.782,-0.123]$, and $[+0.171,+1.515]$. Both analyses give the same direction for these comparisons. The intervals describe evaluation-sample uncertainty for a single training seed; they do not estimate training-seed variation and are not adjusted for multiple comparisons. The GENERIC--B0 comparison changes both inputs and trainable parameters, so its positive difference cannot be attributed to geometry alone.

\subsection{Task and Reasoning-Level Analysis}
\input{sec/results_levels}
\input{sec/results_tasks}
The 30K Full task scores show opposing effects (Table~\ref{tab:tasks}). Relative to B0, CGMF gains 11.735 points on view-change inference and 5.588 on object--object center-distance inference. It loses 19.338 points on position matching and 5.500 on camera-motion inference. GENERIC gains 7.405 points on object--camera distance prediction, but loses 7.379 on position matching. These are descriptive task differences; they are not claims of task-specific significance after multiple-comparison correction.

At 30K Full, CGMF scores 44.254, 30.176, and 58.449 for Low, Middle, and High, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:levels}). B0 scores 45.301, 34.544, and 58.194, while GENERIC scores 47.120, 33.144, and 58.945. The Middle aggregate combines a gain on view-change inference with losses on matching and camera motion. The small High difference relative to B0 coexists with lower Low and Middle scores. Thus access to measured geometry does not produce a uniform improvement across spatial tasks, and the group averages do not identify the cause of an individual task change.

\subsection{Input Interventions and Remaining Ablations}
\label{sec:interventions}
We evaluate input substitutions at the 25K CGMF checkpoint on the same 600 development samples (Table~\ref{tab:interventions}). The development pixel budget is 50,176, so these scores should not be compared directly with Tiny/Full. A separate six-question, six-scene engineering pilot also tested input and latent substitutions, with one example per task. That pilot is insufficient for task-level accuracy or latent-mediation conclusions. The development interventions below provide a larger sensitivity test but do not replace a matched retraining ablation.
\input{sec/results_interventions}

\paragraph{Correct versus wrong pose.}
For each sample, a fixed donor from a different physical scene with the same view count supplies the pose representation. RGB, question, and cached recipient BIP3D features remain unchanged. Overall decreases from 47.037 to 45.913, a correct-minus-wrong difference of 1.123 points with a paired 95\% interval of $[-0.247,+2.593]$. PoseScore decreases from 24.800 to 21.089, a difference of 3.711 points with interval $[+0.044,+7.511]$. The pose-task interval suggests input sensitivity, whereas the Overall interval includes zero. Because the recipient BIP3D features still depend on their original calibration, this intervention tests the direct pose branch rather than removing all camera information.

\paragraph{Correct versus wrong geometry.}
The fixed donor mapping instead supplies cached geometric features and their validity mask, while recipient RGB, question, and pose embeddings remain unchanged. Overall decreases from 47.037 to 45.783, a difference of 1.253 points with interval $[-0.143,+2.693]$. PoseScore decreases from 24.800 to 23.556, a difference of 1.244 points with interval $[+0.511,+2.089]$. Both Overall intervention intervals include zero. Donor features change appearance, depth, and position information together, so these tests do not isolate numerical geometry or show that correct geometry improves performance over B0 or GENERIC. The tests do not identify whether the effect passes through the continuous latents.

\paragraph{K/V and gate conditioning.}
The completed CGMF--GENERIC comparison changes camera-conditioned K/V biasing and camera-conditioned gating jointly. Separate K/V-only and gate-only retraining ablations remain necessary to distinguish their effects. Such comparisons should preserve the other inputs, parameter budget, schedule, and seed initialization. Inference-time disabling would measure a different intervention and should be reported separately.

\paragraph{Latent-related analysis.}
The present architecture preserves eight recurrent latent steps. The six-scene pilot does not provide stable evidence of latent mediation. Larger paired activation-substitution experiments are needed to distinguish the direct visual-prefix pathway from the pathway through latent states. Nonzero residual scales, altered attention, or changed hidden states alone do not show improved spatial reasoning.

\subsection{Limitations and Scope}
The results cover one training seed and a follow-up continuation, with incomplete source-code provenance for the historical B0 trajectory. CGMF and GENERIC receive measured depth and calibration and train more parameters than B0. Question-independent sampling can omit relevant object detail. The fusion module does not enforce point correspondences, deterministic geometric constraints, or 3D equivariance. Its RGB-D features combine appearance and geometric information, which limits causal interpretation of donor substitutions. Additional extraction and fusion computation is required despite the unchanged language input length; latency and memory advantages have not been measured. No VLA simulation or real-robot experiments have been conducted. Multi-seed replication, separate conditioning ablations, and calibration-robustness tests are needed to determine how broadly the observed task tradeoffs apply.
