\begin{abstract}
Can externally grounded explicit geometry improve implicit continuous spatial reasoning in vision-language models? We investigate this question with PIGeR, a pose-integrated geometric reasoning system built on 3DThinker. PIGeR combines measured relative camera poses with frozen RGB-D features from BIP3D and adapts the camera-guided modality fusion (CGMF) module of SpaceMind to enhance visual tokens before language processing. A zero-initialized residual scale preserves the original visual prefix at initialization. The language model retains its eight recurrent continuous latents; geometry reaches them through the visual prefix without additional input tokens or direct geometry-to-latent attention. We compare camera-guided fusion against an equal-parameter generic fusion control that retains the same geometric and camera inputs. Verified single-seed, 10,000-step SPAR-Bench results yield Full macro scores of 41.899 for camera-guided fusion, 41.439 for generic fusion, and 42.348 for the historical 3DThinker baseline. These interim results do not establish an overall improvement over the baseline or a statistically resolved advantage over generic fusion. We document the integration, category-level tradeoffs, and a controlled evaluation protocol. Later checkpoints and causal input interventions remain pending; the present evidence supports a reproducible investigation rather than a claim of improved spatial reasoning.
\end{abstract}
